Give AI a badge,
not a master key.

AI assistants like Claude can now create tickets, post messages and read code. mcp-hub is the front desk that decides what each person's AI may do, and writes down everything it does. Think of an office building: everyone gets a badge, the badge decides which doors open, and the front desk keeps a log.

1

You prove who you are

You sign in through Keycloak, a standard login system. The AI never sees your password.

2

Your role sets the limits

Viewers can only look. Developers can also create and post. Admins can do bulk jobs. Anything not allowed is refused by default.

3

Every action is checked and logged

Requests are limited to an approved list of repos, projects and channels, and every attempt, allowed or refused, goes in the audit log.

This runs the same role table and allowlist rules in your browser. The real server enforces them against live GitHub, Jira and Slack. Want the technical version? See How it works.

How it works

One choke point,
every call.

Each of the nine tools is a thin wrapper over a single gateway function. A request moves left to right and stops at the first check that refuses it; whatever happens, one redacted row lands in the audit log.

Access

Who can do what

Roles come from the token. A tool that is not listed for a role is refused, and is not even shown to that caller in tools/list.

What the gateway

Every tool call is authenticated, rate limited, authorised and validated before a single upstream request goes out.

Refusals are not failures — they are the control plane doing its job, and they are recorded with the same detail as successes.
